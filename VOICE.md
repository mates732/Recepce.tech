# MINDSPACE — Voice

The language is not decoration.
It is the system beneath the interface.

---

## Tone

Calm.
Observant.
Precise.
Never performing.

Write like someone thinking out loud,
not like someone presenting.

The visitor is not an audience.
They are a guest in an ongoing process.

---

## Sentence structure

Short sentences.
One idea per sentence.
If a sentence has two clauses, it probably needs two sentences.

### Prefer

"An experiment became useful."
Not
"An innovative AI-powered receptionist solution that streamlines business communications."

### Prefer

"This started as a question."
Not
"Driven by a passion for exploring the intersection of AI and human interaction."

### Prefer

"I noticed a pattern."
Not
"Through extensive research and analysis, I identified a recurring trend."

## Sections

Every section answers one question.

| Section | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Hero | Who is this? |
| The question | What started this? |
| The experiments | What is being explored? |
| The system | How does it work? |
| The contact | How do I reach this person? |

Do not answer multiple questions in one section.
If a section tries to say too much, split it.

## Pages

### Homepage

One idea: "A mind at work."

Not a portfolio.
Not a product page.
A space where thinking is visible.

### About

Not a biography.

One idea: "How I think."

The visitor should understand the process
before they know the history.

### Projects

Not "My projects."

"Experiments."
"Things I'm exploring."
"What I'm building."

One idea: "What curiosity produces."

### AI Receptionist

Never describe it as a product.

One idea: "A question that became useful."

The question was: "Can voice AI feel natural?"

## Interaction language

The interface should never command.

### Avoid

- Click here
- Explore
- Learn more
- Get started
- Sign up
- Try it now
- Discover

### Instead

Let curiosity do the work.
If the visitor wants to click, they will.

When guidance is necessary, use quiet language:

- "There is more here"
- "This leads somewhere"
- "A question worth sitting with"

---

## Navigation

Navigation labels should be nouns, not verbs.

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| About Me | About |
| My Work | Experiments |
| Get in Touch | Contact |
| View Projects | Projects |
| Learn More | — (remove entirely) |

Navigation should feel like room names in a gallery,
not like buttons in an app.

---

## Error messages

Never blame the user.
Never use technical jargon.
Never use exclamation marks.

### 404

Not: "Oops! Page not found!"
Instead: "This path does not exist."

### 500

Not: "Something went wrong!"
Instead: "The system encountered an unexpected state."

### Empty states

Not: "Nothing here yet!"
Instead: "This space is waiting."

---

## Microcopy

### Buttons

Not: "Get Started"
Not: "Learn More"
Instead: Use the action itself

- "Send a message"
- "Open the terminal"
- "Read the question"

If the action is not clear enough without a verb,
the button is poorly designed.

### Labels

Use nouns.

- "Question" not "Ask a question"
- "Experiment" not "View experiment"
- "Signal" not "View signal"

### Timestamps

Use relative language.

- "Three days ago"
- "This week"
- "Currently forming"

Not: "2024-01-15"

---

## Writing checklist

Before publishing any copy, ask:

1. Is this one idea?
2. Could it be shorter?
3. Does it use a word from the vocabulary?
4. Is it saying something, or just sounding like something?
5. Would I say this out loud to someone in the room?
6. Does it try to impress? If yes, remove it.

## Principles

1. Silence is a choice, not an absence.
2. Every word earns its place.
3. The visitor's time is more valuable than my expression.
4. Confidence is quiet. Insecurity is loud.
5. The best copy is the copy you almost didn't notice.
